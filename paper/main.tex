\begin{abstract}
Robot manipulation policies often treat a last-seen goal as valid until execution fails. That assumption is brittle when a target disappears because it is occluded, moved, removed, temporarily obstructed by a human, invalidated by a task change, or replaceable by a substitute goal. We rebuild Paper 106 around a falsifiable mechanism claim: disappearing-goal manipulation requires explicit goal-validity belief revision, not only active perception, generic uncertainty, or goal retargeting. The local benchmark covers 5 manipulation task families, 7 disappearing-goal regimes, 5 stress splits, 9 methods, 7 seeds, and 84 episodes per group. On combined disappearance stress, the proposed belief-revision method obtains $0.662 \pm 0.009$ success versus $0.561 \pm 0.003$ for the strongest non-oracle baseline, improves goal-validity F1 from $0.439$ to $0.586$, reduces stale-goal pursuit from $0.133$ to $0.087$, and reduces unsafe reach from $0.063$ to $0.039$. Pairwise seed tests favor the proposed method over the strongest baseline in 7/7 seeds, and ablations show that observation-memory separation, active reacquisition, physical-validity testing, substitute-goal planning, and abandonment calibration each matter. A 2026-06-15 continuation audit reran the full benchmark and preserved the terminal decision: \textbf{STRONG\_REVISE}. The local evidence supports the mechanism, but the paper is not ICLR-main-ready until validated on real robots or independent high-fidelity benchmarks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Partial observability is not new. Belief-space task-and-motion planning already represents hidden state and updates plans under uncertainty \citep{kaelbling2013beliefspace,garrett2020online,curtis2024tampura}. Active perception already asks robots to move sensors or viewpoints to reduce uncertainty around occluded targets \citep{kahn2015active}. Goal-conditioned reinforcement learning and Hindsight Experience Replay already exploit alternative achieved goals for sparse-reward learning \citep{andrychowicz2017her}. Modern language-conditioned and multimodal robot policies can condition on task prompts and broad visual context \citep{mees2022calvin,jiang2022vima,brohan2022rt1}.

The problem in this paper is narrower. A disappearing manipulation goal is ambiguous: the goal may be hidden but still valid, moved to a new pose, physically removed, blocked by a temporary human obstacle, invalidated by a changed instruction, or replaceable by a substitute goal. Treating all of these cases as one uncertainty variable causes two different failures. The robot can blindly chase stale goals, increasing unsafe reaches, or it can halt/abandon too early, losing recoverable tasks.

\section{Method sketch}
The proposed method, \texttt{proposed\_disappearing\_goal\_belief\_revision}, maintains separate belief mass over:
\begin{itemize}
\item perceptual disappearance: the goal is unseen but likely still valid;
\item physical invalidation: the goal has been removed, consumed, or made unreachable;
\item motion of the goal: the goal remains valid but its pose or container changed;
\item task-goal change: the commanded goal itself has changed;
\item substitute feasibility: an equivalent or acceptable substitute goal exists;
\item abandonment risk: stopping now may be either safe or unnecessarily conservative.
\end{itemize}

The policy chooses among waiting, active viewpoint reacquisition, retargeting, substitute-goal execution, and abandonment. The implementation is an executable diagnostic benchmark rather than a trained neural policy. That is sufficient for a local mechanism audit, but not sufficient for a final empirical robotics submission.

\section{Benchmark}
The rebuilt benchmark is generated by \texttt{src/run\_experiment.py}. It writes aggregate CSVs, figures, and LaTeX tables directly, so the full run remains lightweight without dropping tasks, regimes, baselines, seeds, ablations, or stress tests.

\paragraph{Tasks.}
The tasks are shelf retrieval, drawer placement, bin sorting, tool handoff, and mobile pick-and-place.

\paragraph{Disappearing-goal regimes.}
The regimes are visual occlusion, object moved, object removed, human temporary obstruction, goal specification changed, substitute goal available, and cascading disappearing goal.

\paragraph{Splits.}
The splits are nominal, occlusion-heavy shift, physical-removal shift, delayed reappearance, and combined disappearance stress.

\paragraph{Methods.}
The baselines are last-seen goal pursuit, memory-only belief tracking, uncertainty halt, active viewpoint reacquisition, POMDP belief planning, goal retargeting heuristic, and failure-aware manipulation policy. The benchmark also includes the proposed method and an oracle goal-state supervisor.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
The evidence reports task success, goal-validity F1, retarget precision, stale-goal pursuit, unsafe reach, false abandonment, reappearance recovery, substitute-goal success, belief-update latency, intervention cost, regret to oracle, paired seed differences, ablations, stress sweeps, and failure cases.

\section{Results}
\input{../results/combined_stress_table.tex}

The strongest non-oracle baseline is \texttt{failure\_aware\_manipulation\_policy}. On combined disappearance stress, the proposed method improves success by $0.102 \pm 0.008$ in paired seed comparisons, wins 7/7 seeds, improves goal-validity F1 by $0.148$, reduces stale-goal pursuit by $0.046$, reduces unsafe reach by $0.024$, and reduces false abandonment by $0.039$. The oracle remains substantially higher at $0.803 \pm 0.007$, so the benchmark is not saturated. The proposed method does increase intervention cost from $0.226$ to $0.247$ relative to the strongest non-oracle baseline; this should be read as a safety and goal-validity tradeoff rather than a free efficiency gain.

At maximum stress level $0.95$, the proposed method remains above the strongest non-oracle baseline with $0.661 \pm 0.007$ success versus $0.547 \pm 0.009$, higher goal-validity F1 ($0.571$ versus $0.422$), lower stale-goal pursuit ($0.088$ versus $0.143$), lower unsafe reach ($0.038$ versus $0.065$), lower false abandonment ($0.054$ versus $0.097$), and lower belief-update latency ($0.700$ versus $0.823$). This is still local evidence rather than external validation.

\section{Where the method still fails}
The failure-case CSV shows that belief revision helps least when active perception can quickly reacquire an occluded goal or when substitute-goal choice is trivial. Those cases are useful boundary tests: the proposed method should not claim superiority over simple active perception when disappearance is purely visual and short-lived. The method's intended value appears when disappearance mixes hidden, invalid, moved, changed, and substitutable goal states.

\section{Hostile prior-work boundary}
Belief-space TAMP and POMDP planning already handle hidden state and replanning under uncertainty \citep{kaelbling2013beliefspace,garrett2020online,curtis2024tampura}. Active exploration already addresses information-gathering for occluded manipulation scenes \citep{kahn2015active}. HER and goal-conditioned learning already exploit alternate goals \citep{andrychowicz2017her}. CALVIN, VIMA, and RT-1 demonstrate increasingly general language- or prompt-conditioned robot policy learning \citep{mees2022calvin,jiang2022vima,brohan2022rt1}.

The present paper survives only under a narrower interpretation: it is about \emph{goal-validity belief revision} for disappearing manipulation goals, not partial observability, active perception, retargeting, or goal-conditioned robot learning in general. If real robot experiments later show that POMDP planning, active perception, goal retargeting, or failure-aware manipulation matches the proposed method on success, stale-goal pursuit, unsafe reach, false abandonment, and ablation robustness, the claim should be killed.

\section{Limitations and terminal decision}
The evidence is strong enough to justify continued work but not submission. The limitations are material:
\begin{itemize}
\item the benchmark is local and synthetic;
\item no real robot or independent high-fidelity simulator has been used;
\item baselines are executable diagnostic models, not external trained robot systems;
\item no learned policy checkpoint, training curve, or deployment video is provided;
\item no external benchmark such as LIBERO, RLBench, Meta-World, BridgeData, CALVIN, or a hardware manipulation suite is reported.
\end{itemize}

The terminal decision for this rebuild is therefore \textbf{STRONG\_REVISE}. The next honest version must add real robot or independent high-fidelity evidence, implemented learned baselines, and qualitative rollouts. Without that evidence, the paper should not be submitted to ICLR main.
